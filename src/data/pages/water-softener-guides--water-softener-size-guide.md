## How Do You Size a Water Softener?

Daily grains to remove equal gallons used per day multiplied by hardness in gpg. A common sizing assumption is 75 gallons per person per day <mark class="data-gap">[VERIFY: sizing assumption]</mark>, so a household of four uses about 300 gallons per day. Multiply that by your hardness to get grains per day, and multiply by the days you want between regenerations to get the capacity you need.

## Worked Examples for St George Water

A household of four at 13 gpg needs 300 x 13 = 3,900 grains per day, so a 48,000-grain unit regenerates about every 12 days. At 18 gpg it needs 5,400 grains per day, so a 48,000-grain unit lasts about 9 days. At 24 gpg it needs 7,200 grains per day, so a 48,000-grain unit lasts about 7 days, and a 32,000-grain unit about 4 days. Units are often set to regenerate sooner than this, so the technician sets the schedule at install.

## Iron and Other Adjustments

Iron in the water uses softener capacity, so the technician adds it to the hardness figure before sizing. A common rule adds about 5 gpg for each 1 mg/L of iron <mark class="data-gap">[VERIFY: cite source]</mark>. A [free water test](/water-quality-testing/) gives the iron reading.

<summary>What happens if my softener is too small?</summary>

It regenerates too often, uses extra salt and water, and can run out of capacity during heavy use, so hard water reaches the taps.

Both matter. Grains measure the capacity of the resin, and gallons measure how much water you use each day. The sizing formula multiplies gallons by hardness to get grains.
